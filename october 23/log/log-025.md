# Log 025 — Touch calibration of the table height (right arm on the table, motors off) and what it explains (2026-10-24 15:17 to 15:25)

## Goal
The user saw the arm dragging on the table, but the model said the hand never got closer than 5.8 cm (log-024). Find where the table really is in the model's frame by putting the gripper on it, and say why the dragging happened.

## Method (read-only, nothing moved)
Both arms limp (motors off), station stopped (log-024). The user laid the right gripper on the table. A read-only query (40 reads in 2 s, spread 0.02 deg: steady) gave the right arm's logical joints (deg): -9.2, 11.5, -1.2, 43.5, 5.2, 7.4, 58.9 (`log/stage6-touch-calibration-right-arm-20261024.json`). Then, at exactly this pose, the lowest points of the right arm's meshes of the official URDF (`openarm_description`, the real meshes) were computed in the arm base frame (shoulder = 0).

## Results
- **Dream's model, grasp point (arm frame, cm):** official mapping x 24.6, y -18.7, **z -38.1**; identity mapping x 12.6, y -7.1, z -48.1.
- **Lowest mesh points of the right arm, official URDF:** link 6 and link 5 **-41.8**, link 7 -41.5, the right finger -41.4 cm (the two finger-side parts and the wrist links within 0.4 cm of each other). Several different parts of the arm at one height means the hand really lies flat on one level surface, so **the table top is at z = -41.8 cm in the model frame, 28.0 cm above the base plate** (the shoulder is 69.8 cm above the plate). The tape reading was 25 cm (-44.8 cm): **the table is 3.0 cm higher than assumed.**
- **The grasp point is not the lowest point.** With the hand horizontal the underside of the gripper (link 6, the wrist) is **3.9 cm below the grasp point**.

## Why the arm dragged
Two things added up: (1) the table was 3.0 cm higher than the box assumed, and (2) the planner and the guard keep the grasp point (a point) clear of the box, not the underside of the hand. In run 4 the grasp point hovered 5.8 to 6.4 cm above the (wrong) box top, that is only 2.8 to 3.4 cm above the real surface; the underside, 3.9 cm lower, was then about 0.5 to 1.1 cm **inside** the real table. That is the contact the user saw. The model was not wrong about where the hand was: the table was.

## Mapping confirmed in a bent pose (closes the open point of log-018)
The official URDF with the fork's logical angles explains the touch pose: five parts of the wrist, hand and finger on one plane within 0.4 cm. The identity mapping puts Dream's grasp point at -48.1 cm, 3.3 cm *below* the tape surface and about 10 cm below the mesh model, impossible for a hand resting on the table. This is one pose of one arm, but it is the first physical measurement in a bent pose and it supports the official mapping and rejects identity.

## What was changed (config only, station stopped, arms off)
- Table top corrected to **28.0 cm above the base plate** (touch-measured; was 25 cm from the tape). The near edge (13 cm) and the box size are unchanged tape values, not touch-checked.
- Safety margin **5.0 cm above the true surface** (`--table-lower-cm=-5`): the box top is 33 cm above the plate = **-36.8 cm** in the arm frame. This is the **same box** as the 8 cm margin over the wrong surface applied in log-024 (8 cm over -44.8 is 5 cm over -41.8), so the protection did not change; the files now describe it truthfully (`table_z -0.418`, `table_danger_lowered_cm -5.0`).
- Bundle `workspace/zones/zones-20261024-1523.yaml` (sha256 `b40559b81650`) applied: `zones.yaml` `b40559b81650`; `station.yaml` `c27da0d46256` (unchanged: same box and home). Backups: `station.yaml.20261024-152400.bak` and the earlier ones, rollback copy `~/.dreamlabs/rollback-20261024-1516/`. Preflight: all checks pass.
- With this margin the guard keeps the grasp point at least 2 cm above the box top, that is 7 cm above the true surface: a horizontal hand's underside stays at least about 3.1 cm above the table. A hand pointing down has the fingertips as its lowest point, so it keeps 7 cm as well. **Grasping an object lying on the table is still not possible**: a deliberate trade for safety.
